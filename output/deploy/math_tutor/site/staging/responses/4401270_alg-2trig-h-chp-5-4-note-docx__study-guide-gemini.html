2. Core Definitions, Theorems, and Formulas

Definitions:

  • One-to-One Restriction: To make trig functions invertible, their domains are restricted:
  • Sine is restricted to $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$
  • Cosine is restricted to $[0, \pi]$
  • Tangent is restricted to $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$
  • Inverse Trigonometric Functions: Functions that return the angle that produces a given trigonometric ratio. They are denoted using a $-1$ superscript (e.g., $\sin^{-1} x$) or the "arc" prefix (e.g., $\arcsin x$).

Formulas (Domains & Ranges of Inverse Functions):

  • Inverse Sine ($\sin^{-1} x$ or $\arcsin x$):
  • Domain: $[-1, 1]$
  • Range: $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$
  • Inverse Cosine ($\cos^{-1} x$ or $\arccos x$):
  • Domain: $[-1, 1]$
  • Range: $[0, \pi]$
  • Inverse Tangent ($\tan^{-1} x$ or $\arctan x$):
  • Domain: All Real Numbers ($\mathbb{R}$)
  • Range: $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$

Reciprocal Inverse Identities:

  • $\sec^{-1} x = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ (Document specifies for $x \ge 1$)
  • $\csc^{-1} x = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ (Document specifies for $x \ge 1$)
  • $\cot^{-1} x = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ (Document specifies for $x > 0$)

3. Worked Study Guide: Step-by-Step Concepts

Concept 1: Evaluating Basic Inverse Trig Functions (Example 1) When you see an expression like $\sin^{-1}(\frac{1}{2})$, you are being asked a specific question: "What angle within the restricted range $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$ gives a sine ratio of $\frac{1}{2}$?"

  • Step 1: Recall your unit circle. The angles that yield a sine of $1/2$ are $\pi/6$ and $5\pi/6$.
  • Step 2: Check the allowed range. For $\sin^{-1}$, the answer must be between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$ (Quadrants I and IV).
  • Step 3: Select the correct angle. $\pi/6$ is in Quadrant I, so $\sin^{-1}(1/2) = \pi/6$.

Concept 2: Evaluating Composite Functions Using Right Triangles (Example 5) How to evaluate an expression like $\cos(\tan^{-1}(\frac{4}{3}))$:

  • Step 1: Work from the inside out. Let $\theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{4}{3})$. This translates to $\tan \theta = \frac{4}{3}$.
  • Step 2: Draw a right triangle for $\theta$. Since tangent is $\frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}}$, label the Opposite side $4$ and the Adjacent side $3$. (Because $4/3$ is positive, we assume Quadrant I).
  • Step 3: Use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the hypotenuse: $3^2 + 4^2 = c^2 \Rightarrow 9 + 16 = c^2 \Rightarrow c = 5$.
  • Step 4: Evaluate the outer function. Now you need $\cos \theta$. Cosine is $\frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$, which is $\frac{3}{5}$.

Concept 3: Rewriting as an Algebraic Expression (Example 6) How to rewrite an expression like $\cot(\cos^{-1} x)$:

  • Step 1: Set the inner function to $\theta$: let $\theta = \cos^{-1} x$. Therefore, $\cos \theta = x$, which can be written as $\frac{x}{1}$.
  • Step 2: Draw a right triangle. Cosine is $\frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$. Label the Adjacent side $x$ and the Hypotenuse $1$.
  • Step 3: Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the Opposite side. $x^2 + \text{Opp}^2 = 1^2 \Rightarrow \text{Opp} = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.
  • Step 4: Evaluate the outer function: $\cot \theta = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Opposite}}$. Substitute the sides you found to get $\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$.

Concept 4: Solving Word Problems (Example 7)

  • Step 1: Sketch the scenario. For a 96-foot tree casting a 120-foot shadow, the tree is the vertical side (Opposite), and the shadow is the horizontal side (Adjacent).
  • Step 2: Choose the right trig ratio. We have Opposite and Adjacent, so we use Tangent. $\tan \theta = \frac{96}{120}$.
  • Step 3: Use the inverse function to find the angle. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{96}{120})$. Use a calculator set to degrees to find $\theta \approx 38.7^\circ$.

Problem 2: Use the reciprocal identities provided to rewrite and evaluate the exact value of $\sec^{-1}(2)$ in radians. Problem 3: Find the exact value of the composite function: $\sin(\cos^{-1}(\frac{5}{13}))$. Problem 4: Rewrite the following as an algebraic expression in $x$: $\tan(\sin^{-1} x)$. Problem 5: A 50-foot building casts a shadow that is 50 feet long. What is the exact angle of elevation of the sun in degrees?

Answers: 1. $-\pi/6$ (The sine is negative in Quadrant IV, and we must use the restricted range $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$). 2. $\sec^{-1}(2) = \cos^{-1}(\frac{1}{2}) =$ $\pi/3$. 3. Let $\theta = \cos^{-1}(\frac{5}{13})$, meaning $\cos \theta = \frac{5}{13}$. Draw a triangle with Adjacent = 5, Hypotenuse = 13. By Pythagorean theorem, Opposite = 12. We want $\sin \theta = \frac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Hyp}} =$ $\frac{12}{13}$. 4. Let $\theta = \sin^{-1} x \Rightarrow \sin \theta = \frac{x}{1}$. Draw a triangle with Opposite = $x$, Hypotenuse = 1. By Pythagorean theorem, Adjacent = $\sqrt{1 - x^2}$. We want $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}} =$ $\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$. 5. Let $\theta$ be the angle of elevation. $\tan \theta = \frac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}} = \frac{50}{50} = 1$. Therefore, $\theta = \tan^{-1}(1) =$ $45^\circ$.

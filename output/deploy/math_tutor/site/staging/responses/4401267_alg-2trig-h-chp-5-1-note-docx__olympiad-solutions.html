1. Suppose a circle of radius \( r \) has a sector with central angle \( \theta \) (in radians) whose area equals the arc length of the same sector. Find, in terms of \( r \), all positive values of \( \theta \) that satisfy this property.

Solution.

  • Sector area: \( \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta \)
  • Arc length: \( r \theta \)

Set equal: \[ \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = r \theta \]

If \( \theta > 0 \), divide both sides by \( r \theta \):

\[ \frac{1}{2} r = 1 \implies r = 2 \]

But we’re asked for values of \( \theta \) in terms of \( r \). Alternatively, suppose the area equals the arc length:

\[ \frac{1}{2}r^2 \theta = r \theta \] If \( r \neq 0 \) and \( \theta \neq 0 \), \[ \frac{1}{2} r \theta = \theta \implies r = 2 \]

Thus, for any positive \( \theta \), \( r = 2 \) is required, not the other way.

But perhaps the intended meaning is that, for given \( r \), find all positive \( \theta \) that make the equality hold. So, \[ \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = r \theta \implies \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta - r \theta = 0 \implies r \theta \left( \frac{1}{2} r - 1 \right) = 0 \] Since \( r, \theta > 0 \), this means \( r = 2 \).

Thus, for all positive values of \( \theta \), the equality can only happen if \( r = 2 \).

Or perhaps you want \( \theta \) on both sides: area \( = \) arc length: \[ \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = r \theta' \]

But it asks for values of \( \theta \), so that's clear: no positive value of \( \theta \neq 0 \) works unless \( r = 2 \); if \( r = 2 \), any positive \( \theta \) works.

Answer: \[ \boxed{ \text{All positive values of } \theta,\ \text{but only if } r = 2. } \] If \( r \) is arbitrary and fixed, there are no positive \( \theta \) unless \( r = 2 \), otherwise none.

2. How many distinct positive integers \( n \) are there such that \( n^\circ \) and \( 2n^\circ \) are coterminal angles in standard position?

Solution.

\( n^\circ \) and \( 2n^\circ \) are coterminal if their difference is a multiple of 360°: \[ 2n - n = n \equiv 0 \pmod{360} \implies 360 \mid n \]

So positive integers \( n \leq 360 \) divide 360.

Number of positive divisors of 360:

\( 360 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^1 \), so number of divisors: \[ (3+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24 \]

Answer: \[ \boxed{24} \]

3. The minute and hour hand of a clock are both 10 cm long. At exactly 12:00, both hands are superimposed. After one hour, what is the exact total area traced out by the tips of the hands, treating each path as a sector swept on a circle of radius 10 cm? Express your answer in terms of \( \pi \).

Solution.

  • Minute hand: Completes one full revolution ( \( 2\pi \) radians) in one hour.
  • Hour hand: Moves from 12:00 to 1:00. In 1 hour, it moves \( \frac{1}{12} \) of a circle, i.e., \( \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6} \) radians.
  • Area traced by the tip = area of sector: \( \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta \), with \( r = 10 \) cm.

For the minute hand (full circle): \[ A_m = \frac{1}{2} (10^2) (2\pi) = 50 \times 2\pi = 100\pi\, \text{cm}^2 \]

For the hour hand: \[ A_h = \frac{1}{2} \times 10^2 \times \frac{\pi}{6} = 50 \times \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{25\pi}{3}\, \text{cm}^2 \]

  • Total area:

\[ 100\pi + \frac{25\pi}{3} = \frac{300\pi + 25\pi}{3} = \frac{325\pi}{3} \]

Answer: \[ \boxed{\dfrac{325\pi}{3} \ \text{cm}^2} \]

4. Let \( A \) and \( B \) be two angles (in degrees), both less than \( 360^\circ \), such that the arc lengths subtended by \( A \) in a circle of radius \( r \) and by \( B \) in a circle of radius \( 2r \) are equal. If \( A:B = 3:1 \), what is the value of \( A \)?

Solution.

  • Arc length for \( A \) on radius \( r \): \( \ell_A = r \cdot (\frac{\pi}{180}A) \)
  • Arc length for \( B \) on radius \( 2r \): \( \ell_B = 2r \cdot (\frac{\pi}{180}B) \)
  • Equate: \( r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}A = 2r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}B \implies A = 2B \)

But, \( A:B = 3:1 \implies A = 3k, B = k \). But above, \( A = 2B \implies 3k = 2k \implies k = 0 \).

This suggests a contradiction. Let's check the equation:

Alternatively, perhaps we should check the equality: \[ r \left( \frac{\pi}{180} A \right) = 2r \left( \frac{\pi}{180} B \right) \implies A = 2B. \] But given \( A:B = 3:1 \), so \( A = 3x, B = x \), thus \( 3x = 2x \implies x=0 \). Contradicts \( x > 0 \). Alternatively, perhaps the correct ratio is "the length subtended by \( A \) in radius \( r \)" to "the length subtended by \( B \) in radius \( 2r \)" is 1:1, but with ratio \( 3:1 \) for the angles.

Is there a misinterpretation? Or perhaps the question expects another approach.

Suppose \( A = 3B \):

Then, \[ r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}A = 2r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}B \implies A = 2B \] But also \( A = 3B \). So the only solution is \( B=0 \).

But both angles must be less than \( 360^\circ \), and positive.

Therefore, perhaps no such value.

Alternatively, perhaps the intended meaning is "if the ratio \( A:B = 3:1 \)", what is \( A \) such that arc lengths are equal.

That is: \[ A = 3x, \ B = x \] Arc length equality: \[ r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}(3x) = 2r \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} (x) \implies 3x = 2x \implies x = 0 \] No positive solution.

Conclusion: The only values are zero, which are excluded. Possibly a typo in the problem.

However, perhaps the question was meant for ratios \( 1:3 \) or \( 2:1 \). In the current wording, no positive solution exists.

5. A Ferris wheel with radius 15 m completes one full counterclockwise revolution every 40 seconds. Another identical Ferris wheel next to it rotates in the opposite direction, making exactly \( n \) revolutions during the same amount of time. If the points at the topmost positions of the wheels align every 10 seconds, what is the value of \( n \)?

Solution.

Let their angular speeds be \( \omega_1 = \frac{2\pi}{40} = \frac{\pi}{20} \) (counterclockwise), \( \omega_2 = -n \frac{2\pi}{40} = -\frac{n\pi}{20} \) (clockwise, negative).

Topmost point alignment means their angular displacement sum equals an integer multiple of \( 2\pi \):

Let \( t \) be the time elapsed (in seconds), at which their top points align: \[ \omega_1 t + |\omega_2| t = 2\pi k,\quad \text{for some integer } k \] Here, \( |\omega_2| = \frac{n\pi}{20} \).

\[ (\frac{\pi}{20} + \frac{n\pi}{20}) t = 2\pi k \implies \frac{(1+n)\pi}{20} t = 2\pi k \implies (1 + n) t = 40 k \]

Given that this happens every \( t = 10 \) seconds, so the smallest such \( t \):

Set \( t = 10 \), \[ (1+n) \cdot 10 = 40k \implies (1+n) = 4k \] Both \( n \) and \( k \) are positive integers, and in 40 seconds, the second wheel must complete exactly \( n \) revolutions (\( n \) integer, \( t=40 \) is a period).

But with \( t=10 \), \( (1+n) = 4k \rightarrow n = 4k-1 \), for integer \( k \geq 1 \).

Now, \( t = 40 \) seconds must also be a time at which alignment occurs, i.e. \( 40 \) must be a multiple of \( 10 \).

Let’s check what value of \( n \) makes \( n \) integer when \( t = 40 \):

\[ n = 4k - 1 \] Set \( t = 40 \): this must align; what integer \( k \) yields that the second wheel's number of revolutions is an integer in 40 seconds? Use the other constraint:

In 40 seconds, second wheel rotates \( n \) times, so \( n \) is integer.

Let us check \( k = 1,2,3,4\):

  • \( k=1 \): \( n=3 \)
  • \( k=2 \): \( n=7 \)
  • \( k=3 \): \( n=11 \)
  • \( k=4 \): \( n=15 \)

Since \( n \) must also result in the wheels aligning every 10 seconds, and the period is 40 seconds:

Let’s check if \( n = 3 \) satisfies this:

For \( n = 3 \):

  • In 40 s, second wheel makes 3 revolutions (as required).
  • Every 10 s, \( t = 10 \), \( (1+3)\cdot10 = 40 \), so \( k=1 \), so yes, every 10 s they align.

Thus,

Answer: \[ \boxed{3} \]

6. The area of a sector of a circle is numerically equal (in the same units) to five times the circumference of the circle. Find the central angle of the sector in radians, in terms of \( r \).

Solution.

  • Circumference: \( 2\pi r \)
  • Sector area: \( \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta \)

Given: \[ \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = 5 \cdot 2\pi r = 10\pi r \]

Divide both sides by \( r \) (\( r > 0 \)): \[ \frac{1}{2} r \theta = 10\pi \implies r \theta = 20\pi \implies \theta = \frac{20\pi}{r} \]

Answer: \[ \boxed{\theta = \dfrac{20\pi}{r}} \]

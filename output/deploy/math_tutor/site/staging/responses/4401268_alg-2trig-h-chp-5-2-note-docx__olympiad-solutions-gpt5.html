Solutions

1. Evaluate exactly:

\[ \frac{\sin 15^\circ+\cos 15^\circ}{\sin 15^\circ\cos 15^\circ}. \]

Use \[ \sin x+\cos x=\sqrt2\cos(x-45^\circ), \] so at \(x=15^\circ\), \[ \sin15^\circ+\cos15^\circ=\sqrt2\cos30^\circ=\sqrt2\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=\frac{\sqrt6}{2}. \]

Also, \[ \sin15^\circ\cos15^\circ=\frac12\sin30^\circ=\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac14. \]

Therefore \[ \frac{\sin15^\circ+\cos15^\circ}{\sin15^\circ\cos15^\circ} =\frac{\frac{\sqrt6}{2}}{\frac14} =2\sqrt6. \]

\[ \boxed{2\sqrt6} \]

2. A right triangle has acute angle \(\theta\) with

\[ \tan\theta+\cot\theta=4. \] Find the exact value of \(\sin\theta+\cos\theta\).

We have \[ \tan\theta+\cot\theta =\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}+\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} =\frac{\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} =\frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}. \] Hence \[ \frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}=4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sin\theta\cos\theta=\frac14. \]

Now \[ (\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^2 =\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta+2\sin\theta\cos\theta =1+2\cdot\frac14 =\frac32. \] Since \(\theta\) is acute, \(\sin\theta+\cos\theta>0\). Thus \[ \sin\theta+\cos\theta=\sqrt{\frac32}=\frac{\sqrt6}{2}. \]

\[ \boxed{\frac{\sqrt6}{2}} \]

3. Two observers stand on the same straight road on opposite sides of a tower. Their distances from the tower differ by \(40\) m, and the angles of elevation to the top are \(45^\circ\) and \(30^\circ\). Find the height of the tower.

Let the tower height be \(h\). Let the distances of the two observers from the tower be \(x\) and \(y\), with angles of elevation \(45^\circ\) and \(30^\circ\) respectively.

Since they are on opposite sides, the distances are simply horizontal distances from the tower, and we are given \[ |y-x|=40. \]

From the \(45^\circ\) observer, \[ \tan45^\circ=\frac{h}{x}=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=h. \]

From the \(30^\circ\) observer, \[ \tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad y=h\sqrt3. \]

Because \(\sqrt3>1\), we have \[ y-x=h(\sqrt3-1)=40. \] So \[ h=\frac{40}{\sqrt3-1} =\frac{40(\sqrt3+1)}{3-1} =20(\sqrt3+1). \]

Thus the height of the tower is \[ \boxed{20(\sqrt3+1)\text{ m}}. \]

4. Without a calculator, determine the exact value of

\[ \left(\sec 30^\circ-\tan 30^\circ\right)\left(\sec 30^\circ+\tan 30^\circ\right) +\left(\csc 45^\circ-\cot 45^\circ\right)^2. \]

First term: \[ (\sec30^\circ-\tan30^\circ)(\sec30^\circ+\tan30^\circ) =\sec^2 30^\circ-\tan^2 30^\circ. \] Using the identity \(\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1\), this equals \[ 1. \]

Second term: \[ \csc45^\circ=\sqrt2,\qquad \cot45^\circ=1, \] so \[ (\csc45^\circ-\cot45^\circ)^2=(\sqrt2-1)^2=2-2\sqrt2+1=3-2\sqrt2. \]

Adding, \[ 1+(3-2\sqrt2)=4-2\sqrt2. \]

\[ \boxed{4-2\sqrt2} \]

5. In a right triangle, the altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into segments of lengths \(1\) and \(4\). Find the exact value of the triangle’s perimeter.

Let the right triangle have hypotenuse \(c\), and let the altitude to the hypotenuse divide it into segments \(1\) and \(4\). Then \[ c=1+4=5. \]

A standard right-triangle altitude theorem says: if the hypotenuse is divided into segments \(m\) and \(n\), then the legs satisfy \[ a^2=cm,\qquad b^2=cn. \] Here \(m=1\), \(n=4\), \(c=5\). Thus \[ a^2=5\cdot1=5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a=\sqrt5, \] \[ b^2=5\cdot4=20 \quad\Rightarrow\quad b=2\sqrt5. \]

Therefore the perimeter is \[ a+b+c=\sqrt5+2\sqrt5+5=3\sqrt5+5. \]

\[ \boxed{5+3\sqrt5} \]

6. A balloon is vertically above a straight road. The angles of depression to two mileposts on the same side of the balloon are \(45^\circ\) and \(30^\circ\). How high is the balloon above the road?

Let the balloon’s height above the road be \(h\). Let the nearer and farther mileposts be at horizontal distances \(x\) and \(y\) from the point on the road directly below the balloon.

Since the angles of depression equal the corresponding angles of elevation, we have \[ \tan45^\circ=\frac{h}{x}\Rightarrow x=h, \] and \[ \tan30^\circ=\frac{h}{y}\Rightarrow y=h\sqrt3. \]

The two mileposts are consecutive, so they are \(1\) mile apart. Since they are on the same side, \[ y-x=1. \] Hence \[ h\sqrt3-h=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h(\sqrt3-1)=1 \] and therefore \[ h=\frac{1}{\sqrt3-1} =\frac{\sqrt3+1}{2}. \]

So the balloon is \[ \boxed{\frac{\sqrt3+1}{2}\text{ miles}} \] above the road.

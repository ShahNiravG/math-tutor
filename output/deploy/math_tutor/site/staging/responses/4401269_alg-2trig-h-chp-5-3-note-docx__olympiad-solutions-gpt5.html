1. Find the exact value of \[ \sin 255^\circ+\cos 345^\circ+\tan 225^\circ. \]

Use angle-sum identities and reference angles.

\[ \sin 255^\circ=\sin(180^\circ+75^\circ)=-\sin 75^\circ, \] \[ \cos 345^\circ=\cos(360^\circ-15^\circ)=\cos 15^\circ, \] \[ \tan 225^\circ=\tan(180^\circ+45^\circ)=\tan 45^\circ=1. \]

Now compute: \[ \sin 75^\circ=\sin(45^\circ+30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12 =\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}, \] so \[ \sin 255^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}. \]

Also, \[ \cos 15^\circ=\cos(45^\circ-30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12 =\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}. \]

Hence these two terms cancel: \[ \sin 255^\circ+\cos 345^\circ=0. \]

Therefore, \[ \sin 255^\circ+\cos 345^\circ+\tan 225^\circ=0+1=1. \]

\[ \boxed{1} \]

2. An angle \(\theta\) satisfies \(\sin\theta+\cos\theta=\frac13\), and \(\theta\) lies in Quadrant II. Find the exact value of \(\tan\theta\).

Let \[ s=\sin\theta,\qquad c=\cos\theta. \] We are given \[ s+c=\frac13. \]

Square both sides: \[ (s+c)^2=s^2+2sc+c^2=\frac19. \] Since \(s^2+c^2=1\), \[ 1+2sc=\frac19 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2sc=-\frac89 \quad\Rightarrow\quad sc=-\frac49. \]

Now \(s\) and \(c\) are roots of \[ t^2-\frac13 t-\frac49=0. \] Multiply by \(9\): \[ 9t^2-3t-4=0. \] Thus \[ t=\frac{3\pm \sqrt{9+144}}{18} =\frac{3\pm 3\sqrt{17}}{18} =\frac{1\pm \sqrt{17}}{6}. \]

In Quadrant II, \(\sin\theta>0\) and \(\cos\theta<0\). Hence \[ \sin\theta=\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}{6},\qquad \cos\theta=\frac{1-\sqrt{17}}{6}. \]

Therefore \[ \tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} =\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}{1-\sqrt{17}}. \] Rationalizing, \[ \tan\theta=\frac{(1+\sqrt{17})^2}{1-17} =\frac{18+2\sqrt{17}}{-16} =-\frac{9+\sqrt{17}}{8}. \]

\[ \boxed{-\frac{9+\sqrt{17}}{8}} \]

3. Without using a calculator, determine the sign and exact value of \[ \sec\!\left(-\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)+\csc\!\left(\frac{11\pi}{6}\right). \]

First simplify each term.

Since cosine is even, \[ \cos\left(-\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)=\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{6}\right). \] Now \[ \frac{7\pi}{6}=\pi+\frac{\pi}{6}, \] so \[ \cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)=-\cos\frac{\pi}{6}=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}. \] Thus \[ \sec\left(-\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{-\sqrt3/2}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt3}=-\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}. \]

Next, \[ \frac{11\pi}{6}=2\pi-\frac{\pi}{6}, \] so \[ \sin\left(\frac{11\pi}{6}\right)=-\sin\frac{\pi}{6}=-\frac12. \] Hence \[ \csc\left(\frac{11\pi}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{-1/2}=-2. \]

Therefore \[ \sec\!\left(-\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)+\csc\!\left(\frac{11\pi}{6}\right) =-\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}-2. \]

This is clearly negative.

\[ \boxed{-2-\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}} \] and the sign is \(\boxed{\text{negative}}\).

4. A point \(P(x,y)\) lies on the terminal side of an angle \(\theta\) in Quadrant III, and \[ x+y=-7,\qquad \frac{x}{y}=\cot\theta=\frac34. \] Find the exact values of \(\sin\theta,\cos\theta,\tan\theta\).

We are given \[ \frac{x}{y}=\frac34. \] So \(x: y = 3:4\). Since the point is in Quadrant III, both \(x\) and \(y\) are negative. Write \[ x=-3k,\qquad y=-4k \] for some positive \(k\).

Using \(x+y=-7\), \[ -3k-4k=-7 \Rightarrow -7k=-7 \Rightarrow k=1. \] Hence \[ x=-3,\qquad y=-4. \]

Now \[ r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=\sqrt{9+16}=5. \]

Therefore \[ \sin\theta=\frac{y}{r}=-\frac45,\qquad \cos\theta=\frac{x}{r}=-\frac35,\qquad \tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}=\frac{-4}{-3}=\frac43. \]

\[ \boxed{\sin\theta=-\frac45,\quad \cos\theta=-\frac35,\quad \tan\theta=\frac43} \]

5. Two sides of a triangle have lengths \(13\) and \(20\), and the included angle is \(150^\circ\). Find the area of the triangle.

Use the formula \[ \text{Area}=\frac12 ab\sin C. \] Here \(a=13\), \(b=20\), and \(C=150^\circ\). Thus \[ \text{Area}=\frac12(13)(20)\sin 150^\circ. \] Since \[ \sin 150^\circ=\sin 30^\circ=\frac12, \] we get \[ \text{Area}=130\cdot\frac12=65. \]

\[ \boxed{65} \]

6. An observer is \(5280\) ft from the launch pad of a rocket. At one moment the angle of elevation is \(45^\circ\); one minute later it is \(60^\circ\). Assuming the rocket rises straight up at constant speed during that minute, how many feet does it rise?

Let the horizontal distance from observer to launch pad be \[ d=5280\text{ ft}. \] If the rocket’s height is \(h\), then \[ \tan\theta=\frac{h}{d}. \]

At the first moment, \(\theta=45^\circ\): \[ h_1=5280\tan45^\circ=5280. \]

One minute later, \(\theta=60^\circ\): \[ h_2=5280\tan60^\circ=5280\sqrt3. \]

So the rise during that minute is \[ h_2-h_1=5280(\sqrt3-1). \]

\[ \boxed{5280(\sqrt3-1)\text{ ft}} \]

1. Let $\theta_n = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)$. Evaluate the exact value of the product: $$ \cos(\theta_1) \cdot \cos(\theta_2) \cdot \cos(\theta_3) \cdots \cos(\theta_{99}) $$

Solution: If $\theta_n = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)$, we can visualize a right triangle with an opposite side of length $1$ and an adjacent side of length $\sqrt{n}$. By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{1^2 + (\sqrt{n})^2} = \sqrt{n+1}$. Thus, the cosine of the angle is given by: $$ \cos(\theta_n) = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n+1}} $$ The given expression transforms into a telescoping product: $$ \prod_{n=1}^{99} \cos(\theta_n) = \frac{\sqrt{1}}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{4}} \cdots \frac{\sqrt{99}}{\sqrt{100}} $$ Canceling all the intermediate numerator-denominator pairs leaves only the first numerator and the final denominator: $$ \frac{\sqrt{1}}{\sqrt{100}} = \frac{1}{10} $$

2. Evaluate the exact value of the expression below (where the angle $5$ is measured in radians): $$ \sin^{-1}(\sin 5) + \cos^{-1}(\cos 5) $$

Solution: Because $5$ radians falls in the fourth quadrant (since $\frac{3\pi}{2} \approx 4.71 < 5 < 2\pi \approx 6.28$), we can find an acute reference angle $\alpha = 2\pi - 5$ which lies in the interval $(0, \frac{\pi}{2})$. In the fourth quadrant, sine is negative and cosine is positive. Therefore: $$ \sin 5 = -\sin(2\pi - 5) = -\sin \alpha $$ $$ \cos 5 = \cos(2\pi - 5) = \cos \alpha $$ Applying the inverse functions, which evaluate principal values: $$ \sin^{-1}(\sin 5) = \sin^{-1}(-\sin \alpha) = -\alpha = 5 - 2\pi $$ $$ \cos^{-1}(\cos 5) = \cos^{-1}(\cos \alpha) = \alpha = 2\pi - 5 $$ Adding the two results: $$ (5 - 2\pi) + (2\pi - 5) = 0 $$

3. Evaluate the exact value of the following nested composition, expressing your final answer in radians: $$ \cos^{-1}\left( \sin\left( \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) + \tan^{-1}(1) \right) \right) $$

Solution: First, evaluate the standard inverse tangents: $$ \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6} \quad \text{and} \quad \tan^{-1}(1) = \frac{\pi}{4} $$ The expression inside the sine function becomes their sum: $$ \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{12} $$ We now need to evaluate $\cos^{-1}\left(\sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right)\right)$. Utilizing the elegant cofunction identity $\sin x = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$: $$ \sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{12}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{5\pi}{12}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) $$ Because $\frac{\pi}{12}$ falls strictly within the principal domain of the inverse cosine function $[0, \pi]$, we have: $$ \cos^{-1}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right)\right) = \frac{\pi}{12} $$

4. Using the reciprocal properties of inverse trigonometric functions, evaluate the exact value of: $$ \sin\left(\sec^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{4}\right) + \csc^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)\right) $$

Solution: Applying the reciprocal properties $\sec^{-1}(x) = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ and $\csc^{-1}(x) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$, the expression becomes: $$ \sin\left(\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{5}\right) + \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)\right) $$ Notice that both angles describe the same standard $3$-$4$-$5$ right triangle. Let $\alpha = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)$. This implies $\sin \alpha = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{4}{5}$, which means $\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)$ is also equal to $\alpha$. The expression neatly reduces to: $$ \sin(\alpha + \alpha) = \sin(2\alpha) $$ Using the sine double-angle formula: $$ \sin(2\alpha) = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha = 2\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)\left(\frac{4}{5}\right) = \frac{24}{25} $$

5. Find the exact positive real number $x$ that satisfies the equation: $$ \sin^{-1}(x) + \sin^{-1}(2x) = \frac{\pi}{2} $$

Solution: Rearrange the equation to isolate $\sin^{-1}(2x)$: $$ \sin^{-1}(2x) = \frac{\pi}{2} - \sin^{-1}(x) $$ Using the complementary identity $\frac{\pi}{2} - \sin^{-1}(x) = \cos^{-1}(x)$, the equation elegantly simplifies to: $$ \sin^{-1}(2x) = \cos^{-1}(x) $$ Take the sine of both sides. By the right triangle equivalence $\sin(\cos^{-1}(x)) = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ for $x > 0$: $$ 2x = \sqrt{1 - x^2} $$ Square both sides to solve for $x$: $$ 4x^2 = 1 - x^2 \implies 5x^2 = 1 $$ Since we are given that $x$ is positive, we take the positive square root: $$ x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5} $$

6. In a right triangle with a hypotenuse of length $10$, the two acute angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ satisfy the equation: $$ \tan^{-1}(\sin \alpha) + \tan^{-1}(\cos \beta) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{8}{15}\right) $$ Find the exact length of the triangle's shortest leg.

Solution: In a right triangle, the acute angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are complementary ($\alpha + \beta = \frac{\pi}{2}$). This intrinsic cofunction relationship dictates that $\cos \beta = \sin \alpha$. Let $x = \sin \alpha$. The given equation simplifies directly to: $$ 2\tan^{-1}(x) = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{8}{15}\right) $$ Take the tangent of both sides and apply the tangent double-angle formula, $\tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$: $$ \frac{2x}{1 - x^2} = \frac{8}{15} $$ Cross-multiplying gives $30x = 8 - 8x^2$, which rearranges into the quadratic equation: $$ 4x^2 + 15x - 4 = 0 $$ Factoring the quadratic reveals $(4x - 1)(x + 4) = 0$. Since $x = \sin \alpha$ must be strictly positive, $x = \frac{1}{4}$. The shortest leg of the right triangle is opposite its smallest angle (which is $\alpha$, since $\sin\alpha < \sin\beta$). Its length is strictly defined by the hypotenuse multiplied by $\sin \alpha$: $$ 10 \sin \alpha = 10 \left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{5}{2} $$

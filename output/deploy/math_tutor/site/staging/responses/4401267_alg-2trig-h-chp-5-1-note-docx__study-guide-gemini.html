1. Document Summary

This document, titled "Chp 5.1: Angle Measure," introduces the foundational concepts of angles in trigonometry. It covers the definition of angles, positive/negative rotation, and standard position. It thoroughly explains the two main systems for measuring angles—degrees and radians—and how to convert between them. Finally, it applies radian measures to geometric and physical scenarios, specifically calculating the length of a circular arc, the area of a circular sector, and the linear and angular speeds of objects in circular motion.

2. Core Definitions, Theorems, and Formulas

Definitions:

  • Angle: Formed by two rays with a common vertex. A rotation from an initial side to a terminal side.
  • Sign of an Angle: Counterclockwise rotation is positive; clockwise rotation is negative.
  • Radian Measure: In a circle of radius 1, the measure of an angle in radians is exactly the length of the arc that subtends the angle.
  • Standard Position: An angle drawn in the $xy$-plane with its vertex at the origin and its initial side on the positive $x$-axis.
  • Coterminal Angles: Two angles in standard position whose terminal sides coincide.
  • Linear Speed ($v$): The rate at which the distance traveled along a circle is changing (distance divided by time).
  • Angular Speed ($\omega$): The rate at which the central angle is changing (number of radians divided by time).

Formulas:

  • Degrees to Radians Conversion: Multiply the degree measure by $\frac{\pi}{180}$.
  • Radians to Degrees Conversion: Multiply the radian measure by $\frac{180}{\pi}$.
  • Length of a Circular Arc ($s$): $s = r\theta$ (where $r$ is radius and $\theta$ must be in radians).
  • Area of a Circular Sector ($A$): $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ (where $r$ is radius and $\theta$ must be in radians).
  • Linear Speed ($v$): $v = \frac{s}{t}$
  • Angular Speed ($\omega$): $\omega = \frac{\theta}{t}$
  • Relationship Between Linear and Angular Speed: $v = r\omega$

3. Worked Study Guide

Here is a step-by-step breakdown of how to think about and use these concepts.

Step 1: Understanding the Radian A radian isn't just an arbitrary number; it relates an angle directly to the radius of a circle. If you wrap the radius of a circle around its outer edge, that arc length subtends exactly 1 radian. Since the full circumference of a circle is $2\pi r$, a full revolution is $2\pi$ radians (which equals $360^\circ$).

Step 2: Converting Between Units Treat $\pi$ radians and $180^\circ$ as your conversion bridge.

  • If you have Degrees and want Radians: You want to cancel degrees, so multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}$.

(Example: $90^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = \frac{\pi}{2}$ radians)

  • If you have Radians and want Degrees: You want to introduce degrees, so multiply by $\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$.

Step 3: Finding Coterminal Angles Because a full circle is $360^\circ$ (or $2\pi$ radians), you can spin around the circle a full turn and end up in the exact same spot.

  • To find coterminal angles in degrees: Add or subtract multiples of $360^\circ$.
  • To find coterminal angles in radians: Add or subtract multiples of $2\pi$.

Step 4: Using the Arc Length and Sector Area Formulas The formulas $s = r\theta$ and $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ are beautifully simple, but they carry a strict rule: $\theta$ MUST be in radians. If a problem gives you an angle like $40^\circ$, your very first step must be converting it to radians ($\frac{2\pi}{9}$ rad) before plugging it into $s = r\theta$ or $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

Step 5: Circular Motion ($\omega$ vs. $v$) Imagine a point on a spinning ceiling fan.

  • Angular speed ($\omega$) tells you how fast the angle is opening up (e.g., 10 radians per second). Every point on the fan blade has the same angular speed.
  • Linear speed ($v$) tells you how fast a specific point is physically traveling through the air. A point on the tip of the blade travels a massive circle, so it moves very fast. A point near the center travels a tiny circle, so it moves slower. The formula $v = r\omega$ connects these ideas: linear speed scales up as the radius gets larger!

4. Practice Problems

Problem 1: Convert $150^\circ$ to radians. Answer: Multiply by $\frac{\pi}{180}$. $150 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{150\pi}{180} = \mathbf{\frac{5\pi}{6} \text{ radians}}$.

Problem 2: Find one positive and one negative coterminal angle for $\frac{\pi}{4}$ rad. Answer: Add and subtract $2\pi$ (which is $\frac{8\pi}{4}$). Positive: $\frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{8\pi}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{9\pi}{4}}$. Negative: $\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{8\pi}{4} = \mathbf{-\frac{7\pi}{4}}$.

Problem 3: A circle has a radius of 5 meters. Find the length of the arc that subtends a central angle of $1.5$ radians. Answer: Use $s = r\theta$. Since the angle is already in radians, simply multiply. $s = 5 \times 1.5 = \mathbf{7.5 \text{ meters}}$.

Problem 4: Find the area of a circular sector if the radius is 6 cm and the central angle is $60^\circ$. Answer: First, convert $60^\circ$ to radians: $60 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{3}$. Next, use $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$. $A = \frac{1}{2}(6)^2(\frac{\pi}{3}) = \frac{1}{2}(36)(\frac{\pi}{3}) = 18(\frac{\pi}{3}) = \mathbf{6\pi \text{ cm}^2}$.

Problem 5: A wheel with a radius of 10 inches is spinning at an angular speed of $3 \text{ rad/sec}$. What is the linear speed of a point on the outer edge of the wheel? Answer: Use the relationship $v = r\omega$. $v = 10 \text{ in} \times 3 \text{ rad/sec} = \mathbf{30 \text{ in/sec}}$. (Note: "radians" drop out as a unit when converting to linear distance).

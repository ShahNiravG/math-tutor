2. Core Definitions, Theorems, and Formulas

Core Definitions

  • Inverse Sine ($\arcsin$): $y = \sin^{-1} x \iff \sin y = x$.
  • Domain: $[-1, 1]$
  • Range: $[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$
  • Inverse Cosine ($\arccos$): $y = \cos^{-1} x \iff \cos y = x$.
  • Domain: $[-1, 1]$
  • Range: $[0, \pi]$
  • Inverse Tangent ($\arctan$): $y = \tan^{-1} x \iff \tan y = x$.
  • Domain: All real numbers ($\mathbb{R}$)
  • Range: $(-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$
  • Inverse Reciprocal Functions: To define inverses for secant, cosecant, and cotangent, their original domains are restricted to sets where they are strictly one-to-one. Based on the provided graphs:
  • $\sec^{-1} x$ has a range of $[0, \frac{\pi}{2}) \cup [\pi, \frac{3\pi}{2})$
  • $\csc^{-1} x$ has a range of $(0, \frac{\pi}{2}] \cup (\pi, \frac{3\pi}{2}]$
  • $\cot^{-1} x$ has a range of $(0, \pi)$

Theorems: Cancellation Properties These formulas allow you to "cancel" a trig function with its inverse, provided the input falls within the correct restricted interval.

  • Sine:
  • $\sin(\sin^{-1} x) = x$ for $-1 \le x \le 1$
  • $\sin^{-1}(\sin x) = x$ for $-\frac{\pi}{2} \le x \le \frac{\pi}{2}$
  • Cosine:
  • $\cos(\cos^{-1} x) = x$ for $-1 \le x \le 1$
  • $\cos^{-1}(\cos x) = x$ for $0 \le x \le \pi$
  • Tangent:
  • $\tan(\tan^{-1} x) = x$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$
  • $\tan^{-1}(\tan x) = x$ for $-\frac{\pi}{2} < x < \frac{\pi}{2}$

3. Worked Study Guide

To master this material, you need to understand how to apply the definitions and cancellation properties to solve the examples on the final page of the document. Here is a step-by-step guide to the core concepts.

Concept A: Finding Exact Values of Inverse Trig Functions When asked to evaluate an expression like $\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$, you are being asked: "What angle $y$ in the interval $[0, \pi]$ gives a cosine ratio of $-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$?" 1. Identify the reference angle: We know $\cos(\frac{\pi}{6}) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. 2. Check the sign and range: The ratio is negative. Cosine is negative in Quadrants II and III. However, the range of $\cos^{-1}x$ is strictly $[0, \pi]$ (Quadrants I and II). 3. Find the Quadrant II angle: $\pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$. 4. Result: $\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \frac{5\pi}{6}$.

Concept B: Using Cancellation Properties (Beware the Traps!) Sometimes you are given a composition like $\cos^{-1}\left(\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)\right)$.

  • The Trap: You might be tempted to just cancel the cosines and say the answer is $\frac{7\pi}{6}$.
  • The Rule: Look at the cancellation properties! $\cos^{-1}(\cos x) = x$ only if $0 \le x \le \pi$. The angle $\frac{7\pi}{6}$ is larger than $\pi$, so you cannot just cancel.
  • The Fix: Evaluate from the inside out.

1. First, find $\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{6}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. 2. Next, find $\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$, which we found in Concept A to be $\frac{5\pi}{6}$. 3. Result: $\cos^{-1}\left(\cos\left(\frac{7\pi}{6}\right)\right) = \frac{5\pi}{6}$.

Concept C: Evaluating when the Value is Undefined Consider $\sin(\sin^{-1} 5)$.

  • Look at the cancellation property: $\sin(\sin^{-1} x) = x$ only if $-1 \le x \le 1$.
  • Because 5 is outside the domain of the inverse sine function (you cannot have a sine ratio greater than 1), $\sin^{-1}(5)$ is undefined. The whole expression is undefined.

Concept D: Rewriting as an Algebraic Expression How do you rewrite an expression like $\cos(\sin^{-1} x)$ without trig functions? Use right triangle trigonometry! 1. Let $\theta = \sin^{-1} x$. By definition, this means $\sin \theta = x$. 2. Write $x$ as a fraction: $\sin \theta = \frac{x}{1} = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$. 3. Draw a right triangle with angle $\theta$. Label the opposite side $x$ and the hypotenuse $1$. 4. Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the adjacent side: $\text{adj}^2 + x^2 = 1^2 \implies \text{adj} = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$. 5. Now evaluate the outside function, $\cos \theta$. Cosine is $\frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}$. 6. Result: $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}{1} = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$.

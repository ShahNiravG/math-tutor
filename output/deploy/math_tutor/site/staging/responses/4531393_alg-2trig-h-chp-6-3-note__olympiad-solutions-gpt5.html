1. Find the exact maximum value of

\[ \sin x+\cos x+\sin 2x \] for real \(x\).

Let \[ S=\sin x+\cos x+\sin 2x. \] Using \(\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x\), write \[ S=\sin x+\cos x+2\sin x\cos x. \] Set \[ u=\sin x+\cos x. \] Then \[ u^2=\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x+2\sin x\cos x=1+\sin 2x, \] so \[ \sin 2x=u^2-1. \] Hence \[ S=u+(u^2-1)=u^2+u-1. \] Now \[ u=\sin x+\cos x\in[-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]. \] Since \(u^2+u-1\) is an upward-opening quadratic, its maximum on the interval \([-\sqrt2,\sqrt2]\) occurs at an endpoint. Evaluate: \[ S(\sqrt2)=2+\sqrt2-1=1+\sqrt2, \] \[ S(-\sqrt2)=2-\sqrt2-1=1-\sqrt2. \] Therefore the maximum is \[ \boxed{1+\sqrt2}. \]

This occurs when \(\sin x+\cos x=\sqrt2\), i.e. at \[ x=\frac{\pi}{4}+2\pi k. \]

2. A sinusoid of the form

\[ y=a\cos(kx-b)+c \] has one complete period on \([1,5]\), maximum value \(7\), minimum value \(-1\), and passes through \((1,3)\) and \((3,7)\). Determine one such equation.

From the maximum and minimum values, \[ a=\frac{7-(-1)}2=4,\qquad c=\frac{7+(-1)}2=3. \] So the function has the form \[ y=4\cos(kx-b)+3. \]

Since one complete period occurs on \([1,5]\), the period is \[ T=5-1=4. \] For \(y=\cos(kx-b)\), the period is \(2\pi/k\), so \[ \frac{2\pi}{k}=4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k=\frac{\pi}{2}. \] Thus \[ y=4\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}-b\right)+3. \]

Now use the point \((3,7)\). Since \(7\) is the maximum, \[ 4\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=7 \] gives \[ \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)=1. \] Hence \[ \frac{3\pi}{2}-b=2\pi n. \] Take \(n=0\), so \[ b=\frac{3\pi}{2}. \]

Then \[ y=4\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}-\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)+3. \] Check \((1,3)\): \[ y(1)=4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)+3 =4\cos(-\pi)+3 =-4+3=-1, \] so this does not pass through \((1,3)\). Thus we should interpret \((3,7)\) as a maximum point, but the phase must also fit \((1,3)\), so let us instead use both points directly.

From \((1,3)\): \[ 4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)=0. \] So \[ \frac{\pi}{2}-b=\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi \quad\text{or}\quad -b=n\pi, \] hence \[ b=n\pi. \]

From \((3,7)\): \[ 4\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=7 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)=1. \] Thus \[ \frac{3\pi}{2}-b=2m\pi. \] If \(b=n\pi\), then \[ \frac{3\pi}{2}-n\pi=2m\pi, \] which has no integer solution. So with \(a=4\), no solution arises.

Hence we may take \(a=-4\) instead, since the amplitude is \(|a|=4\). Then \[ y=-4\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}-b\right)+3. \]

Use \((3,7)\): \[ -4\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=7 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)=-1. \] So \[ \frac{3\pi}{2}-b=(2m+1)\pi. \] Take \(m=0\), giving \[ b=\frac{\pi}{2}. \]

Then \[ y=-4\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+3. \] Check \((1,3)\): \[ y(1)=-4\cos 0+3=-1, \] still not correct.

So let us shift the maximum to \(x=3\) and require one full period from \(x=1\) to \(x=5\). Since \(x=1\) and \(x=5\) are corresponding points one period apart, and \(y(1)=3\), \(y(5)=3\) as well. Also \(x=3\) is halfway through the period from \(1\) to \(5\), so the phase should move by \(\pi\) from \(x=1\) to \(x=3\). Since \(y(1)=3\) is the midline and \(y(3)=7\) is the maximum, a sine form is natural: \[ y=4\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}(x-1)\right)+3. \] Convert to cosine: \[ \sin \theta=\cos\left(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\right), \] so \[ y=4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}(x-1)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+3 =4\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}-\pi\right)+3. \]

Check:

  • At \(x=1\): \(\cos(-\pi)=-1\), so \(y=-4+3=-1\), not \(3\).

So instead we want the graph to start on the midline and rise to a maximum after 2 units. Thus \[ y=4\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}(x-1)\right)+3 \] indeed gives \[ y(1)=3,\qquad y(3)=3, \] so that also fails.

To reach a maximum at \(x=3\), we need \[ \frac{\pi}{2}(3-1)=\pi, \] which is not where sine is maximal.

A better choice is to place cosine maximum at \(x=3\): \[ y=4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}(x-3)\right)+3. \] Then \[ y(3)=7, \] and \[ y(1)=4\cos(-\pi)+3=-1. \] Again not correct.

Thus \(x=3\) cannot be a maximum if the period is exactly \(4\) and \(x=1\) is a midline crossing; the data are inconsistent unless “one complete period on \([1,5]\)” means only that the interval contains a period, not necessarily starting at matching phase endpoints.

Take period \(4\), so \(k=\pi/2\), and solve: \[ 4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=3 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)=0, \] so \[ b=n\pi. \] Also \[ 4\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=7 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)=1. \] This has no solution. With \(a=-4\), \[ -4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=3 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-b\right)=0, \] again \(b=n\pi\), and \[ -4\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)+3=7 \Rightarrow \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-b\right)=-1, \] which also has no solution.

Therefore the given data are inconsistent; no such sinusoid exists.

\[ \boxed{\text{There is no equation of the stated form satisfying all conditions.}} \]

3. Compute exactly:

\[ \sin\frac{\pi}{12}+\sin\frac{5\pi}{12}+\sin\frac{9\pi}{12}+\sin\frac{13\pi}{12}+\sin\frac{17\pi}{12}. \]

Write the angles in degrees: \[ 15^\circ,\ 75^\circ,\ 135^\circ,\ 195^\circ,\ 255^\circ. \] So the sum is \[ \sin 15^\circ+\sin 75^\circ+\sin 135^\circ+\sin 195^\circ+\sin 255^\circ. \]

Now use \[ \sin(180^\circ+\theta)=-\sin\theta. \] Thus \[ \sin 195^\circ=-\sin 15^\circ,\qquad \sin 255^\circ=-\sin 75^\circ. \] These cancel the first two terms, leaving \[ \sin 135^\circ=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}. \] Hence the exact value is \[ \boxed{\frac{\sqrt2}{2}}. \]

4. Let

\[ f(x)=x-2\sin x\qquad (0\le x\le 2\pi). \] Without calculus, determine how many solutions the equation \(f(x)=\pi\) has, and locate each solution exactly if possible.

We solve \[ x-2\sin x=\pi, \] or \[ x-\pi=2\sin x. \]

Let \[ t=x-\pi. \] Then \(x=\pi+t\), where \(t\in[-\pi,\pi]\), and since \[ \sin(\pi+t)=-\sin t, \] the equation becomes \[ t+2\sin t=0. \]

Clearly \(t=0\) is a solution, giving \[ x=\pi. \]

We now show it is the only solution. Observe:

  • If \(0<t\le \pi\), then \(\sin t\ge 0\), so

\[ t+2\sin t>0. \] Hence no positive \(t\neq 0\) works.

  • If \(-\pi\le t<0\), write \(t=-u\) with \(u>0\). Then

\[ t+2\sin t=-u-2\sin u<0, \] since \(u>0\) and \(\sin u\ge 0\) for \(0\le u\le \pi\). Hence no negative \(t\) works.

So the only solution is \[ \boxed{x=\pi}. \]

Therefore the equation \(f(x)=\pi\) has exactly \[ \boxed{1} \] solution.

5. Find the least positive period of

\[ g(x)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}\right)+\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{3}\right). \]

The period of \[ \sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{2}\right) \] is \(T_1\) such that \[ \frac{\pi}{2}T_1=2\pi, \] so \[ T_1=4. \]

The period of \[ \cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{3}\right) \] is \(T_2\) such that \[ \frac{\pi}{3}T_2=2\pi, \] so \[ T_2=6. \]

A common period of the sum must be a common multiple of \(4\) and \(6\). The least positive one is \[ \operatorname{lcm}(4,6)=12. \] Hence the least positive period is \[ \boxed{12}. \]

6. For real \(x\), define

\[ h(x)=\cos x+\cos\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)+\cos\left(x+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right). \] Evaluate \(h(x)\), and then use the same idea to evaluate \[ \sin x+\sin\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)+\sin\left(x+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right). \]

Use angle addition: \[ \cos\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\cos x\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}-\sin x\sin\frac{2\pi}{3} =-\frac12\cos x-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\sin x, \] \[ \cos\left(x+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)=\cos x\cos\frac{4\pi}{3}-\sin x\sin\frac{4\pi}{3} =-\frac12\cos x+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\sin x. \] Adding with \(\cos x\), \[ h(x)=\cos x-\frac12\cos x-\frac12\cos x -\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\sin x+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\sin x=0. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{h(x)=0\ \text{for all }x}. \]

Now similarly, \[ \sin\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)=\sin x\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+\cos x\sin\frac{2\pi}{3} =-\frac12\sin x+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos x, \] \[ \sin\left(x+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)=\sin x\cos\frac{4\pi}{3}+\cos x\sin\frac{4\pi}{3} =-\frac12\sin x-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos x. \] Adding with \(\sin x\), \[ \sin x-\frac12\sin x-\frac12\sin x +\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos x-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cos x=0. \] So \[ \boxed{\sin x+\sin\left(x+\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)+\sin\left(x+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)=0}. \]

Thus both sums are identically zero.

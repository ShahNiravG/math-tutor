Solutions

1. Find the least positive angle in degrees coterminal with

\[ \frac{37\pi}{12}-845^\circ. \]

First convert the radian measure to degrees: \[ \frac{37\pi}{12}\cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}=37\cdot 15^\circ=555^\circ. \] So the angle is \[ 555^\circ-845^\circ=-290^\circ. \] To find the least positive coterminal angle, add \(360^\circ\): \[ -290^\circ+360^\circ=70^\circ. \]

\[ \boxed{70^\circ} \]

2. A sector has perimeter \(20\) and central angle \(2\) radians. Find its area.

For a sector of radius \(r\) and angle \(\theta\), the perimeter is \[ 2r+r\theta. \] Here \(\theta=2\), so \[ 2r+2r=4r=20. \] Thus \[ r=5. \]

The area of a sector is \[ \frac12 r^2\theta. \] Hence \[ \text{Area}=\frac12\cdot 25\cdot 2=25. \]

\[ \boxed{25} \]

3. In a circle, the numerical value of the arc length of a sector equals the numerical value of the area of that sector. If the central angle is not \(0\), find it.

Let the radius be \(r\) and the central angle be \(\theta\) radians.

The arc length is \[ s=r\theta, \] and the sector area is \[ A=\frac12 r^2\theta. \]

We are told these numerical values are equal: \[ r\theta=\frac12 r^2\theta. \] Since \(\theta\ne 0\), divide by \(\theta\): \[ r=\frac12 r^2. \] So \[ 2r=r^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad r(r-2)=0. \] Since a circle cannot have radius \(0\), we get \[ r=2. \]

The equation imposes no further restriction on \(\theta\) besides \(\theta\ne 0\). Thus the central angle can be any nonzero radian measure.

\[ \boxed{\theta\text{ may be any nonzero real number (in radians)}} \]

4. A point moves on a circle of radius \(6\) cm. In the first half of a trip it turns through \(150^\circ\); in the second half it turns through \(\frac{5\pi}{6}\) radians. Find the total distance traveled.

Distance traveled along a circle is arc length: \[ s=r\theta, \] with \(\theta\) in radians.

Convert \(150^\circ\) to radians: \[ 150^\circ=\frac{5\pi}{6}. \] So in the first half, the arc length is \[ 6\cdot \frac{5\pi}{6}=5\pi. \] In the second half, the angle is also \(\frac{5\pi}{6}\), so the arc length is again \[ 6\cdot \frac{5\pi}{6}=5\pi. \]

Therefore the total distance is \[ 5\pi+5\pi=10\pi. \]

\[ \boxed{10\pi\text{ cm}} \]

5. Two angles in standard position satisfy

\[ \alpha+\beta=7\pi,\qquad \alpha-\beta=250^\circ. \] Find the least positive coterminal angle of \(\alpha\).

We first express everything in one unit. Convert \(250^\circ\) to radians: \[ 250^\circ=\frac{250\pi}{180}=\frac{25\pi}{18}. \]

Now solve the system \[ \alpha+\beta=7\pi,\qquad \alpha-\beta=\frac{25\pi}{18}. \] Add the equations: \[ 2\alpha=7\pi+\frac{25\pi}{18} =\frac{126\pi+25\pi}{18} =\frac{151\pi}{18}. \] Hence \[ \alpha=\frac{151\pi}{36}. \]

Now reduce modulo \(2\pi=\frac{72\pi}{36}\): \[ \frac{151\pi}{36}-\frac{72\pi}{36}=\frac{79\pi}{36}, \] and again \[ \frac{79\pi}{36}-\frac{72\pi}{36}=\frac{7\pi}{36}. \] This is positive and less than \(2\pi\), so it is the least positive coterminal angle.

\[ \boxed{\frac{7\pi}{36}} \]

6. A wheel of radius \(r\) rolls without slipping. After exactly \(9\) revolutions, the distance traveled equals the area of a sector of a circle of radius \(r\) and angle \(\theta\) radians. Find \(\theta\).

When a wheel of radius \(r\) rolls without slipping, one revolution covers one circumference: \[ 2\pi r. \] So after \(9\) revolutions, the distance traveled is \[ 9(2\pi r)=18\pi r. \]

The area of a sector of radius \(r\) and angle \(\theta\) is \[ \frac12 r^2\theta. \]

Set these equal: \[ 18\pi r=\frac12 r^2\theta. \] Assuming \(r>0\), divide by \(r\): \[ 18\pi=\frac12 r\theta. \] Thus \[ \theta=\frac{36\pi}{r}. \]

\[ \boxed{\theta=\frac{36\pi}{r}} \]

1. Evaluate the exact value of the following sum: $\log_{2}(\tan 1^\circ) + \log_{2}(\tan 2^\circ) + \log_{2}(\tan 3^\circ) + \dots + \log_{2}(\tan 89^\circ)$

Using the logarithmic identity $\log_a(x) + \log_a(y) = \log_a(xy)$, we can combine the sum into a single logarithm of a product: $$ \log_{2}(\tan 1^\circ \cdot \tan 2^\circ \cdot \tan 3^\circ \dots \tan 89^\circ) $$ Recall the complementary angle identity $\tan(90^\circ - \theta) = \cot \theta$. We can pair each $\tan \theta$ from the first half of the product with $\tan(90^\circ - \theta)$ from the second half: $$ \tan 1^\circ \cdot \tan 89^\circ = \tan 1^\circ \cdot \cot 1^\circ = 1 $$ $$ \tan 2^\circ \cdot \tan 88^\circ = \tan 2^\circ \cdot \cot 2^\circ = 1 $$ This pairing continues for all terms up to $\tan 44^\circ \cdot \tan 46^\circ = 1$. The only unpaired term in the exact middle is $\tan 45^\circ$, which is also equal to $1$. Thus, the entire product evaluates to $1$, leaving us with: $$ \log_2(1) = 0 $$

2. Suppose $x$ is an angle in the interval $(0, \pi)$ such that $\csc x - \cot x = 5$. Determine the exact value of $\cos x$.

We leverage the Pythagorean identity $\csc^2 x - \cot^2 x = 1$. Factoring this as a difference of squares yields: $$ (\csc x - \cot x)(\csc x + \cot x) = 1 $$ Given that $\csc x - \cot x = 5$, we immediately find: $$ \csc x + \cot x = \frac{1}{5} $$ We now have a simple linear system in terms of $\csc x$ and $\cot x$. Subtracting the given equation from our new equation gives: $$ 2\cot x = \frac{1}{5} - 5 = -\frac{24}{5} \implies \cot x = -\frac{12}{5} $$ Adding the two equations yields: $$ 2\csc x = 5 + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{26}{5} \implies \csc x = \frac{13}{5} \implies \sin x = \frac{5}{13} $$ Finally, using the definition $\cot x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$, we multiply to find $\cos x$: $$ \cos x = \cot x \cdot \sin x = \left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)\left(\frac{5}{13}\right) = -\frac{12}{13} $$ (Note: $\cos x < 0$ and $\sin x > 0$ correctly places $x$ in Quadrant II, consistent with $x \in (0, \pi)$.)

3. Find the exact numerical value of the following trigonometric expression: $\sin^6(2024^\circ) + \cos^6(2024^\circ) + 3\sin^2(2024^\circ)\cos^2(2024^\circ)$

Let $\theta = 2024^\circ$. Consider the algebraic identity $(a+b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a+b)$. Let $a = \sin^2 \theta$ and $b = \cos^2 \theta$. By the fundamental Pythagorean identity, we know $a + b = \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$. Substitute $a$ and $b$ into the algebraic identity: $$ (\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)^3 = (\sin^2 \theta)^3 + (\cos^2 \theta)^3 + 3(\sin^2 \theta)(\cos^2 \theta)(\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta) $$ $$ 1^3 = \sin^6 \theta + \cos^6 \theta + 3\sin^2 \theta \cos^2 \theta (1) $$ Therefore, the expression perfectly collapses, completely independent of the angle, to: $$ 1 $$

4. By considering an appropriate trigonometric substitution, determine the maximum possible value of the algebraic function $f(x) = \frac{x}{x^2 + 25}$ over all real numbers $x$.

To simplify the denominator, let $x = 5 \tan \theta$, where $\theta \in (-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2})$. Substituting this into $f(x)$ gives: $$ f(5 \tan \theta) = \frac{5 \tan \theta}{25 \tan^2 \theta + 25} $$ Factor out the $25$ in the denominator and apply the identity $\tan^2 \theta + 1 = \sec^2 \theta$: $$ f(5 \tan \theta) = \frac{5 \tan \theta}{25 \sec^2 \theta} = \frac{1}{5} \left( \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} \cdot \cos^2 \theta \right) = \frac{1}{5} \sin \theta \cos \theta $$ Using the double-angle identity $\sin(2\theta) = 2 \sin \theta \cos \theta$, we can rewrite the function as: $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{10} \sin(2\theta) $$ The sine function attains a maximum value of $1$ (which occurs here when $2\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$, or $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$, corresponding to $x = 5$). Thus, the maximum possible value of $f(x)$ is: $$ \frac{1}{10} $$

5. Evaluate the exact value of the product: $\left( \sin^4(75^\circ) - \cos^4(75^\circ) \right) \sec(150^\circ)$

First, factor the difference of fourth powers inside the parentheses: $$ \sin^4(75^\circ) - \cos^4(75^\circ) = \left( \sin^2(75^\circ) - \cos^2(75^\circ) \right)\left( \sin^2(75^\circ) + \cos^2(75^\circ) \right) $$ By the Pythagorean identity, the second factor is $1$. The first factor resembles the double angle identity for cosine, $\cos(2\theta) = \cos^2 \theta - \sin^2 \theta$. Factoring out a negative sign gives: $$ -(\cos^2(75^\circ) - \sin^2(75^\circ)) = -\cos(2 \times 75^\circ) = -\cos(150^\circ) $$ Now, substitute this back into the original product: $$ \left( -\cos(150^\circ) \right) \sec(150^\circ) $$ Because $\sec \theta$ and $\cos \theta$ are reciprocals ($\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$), their product is $1$. Thus, the expression simplifies to: $$ -1 $$

6. Given the equation $\frac{\sin x + \cos x}{\sin x - \cos x} = 3$, determine the exact value of $\sec^2 x$.

We can transform the given equation entirely in terms of tangent by dividing the numerator and denominator of the fraction by $\cos x$ (noting $\cos x \neq 0$ because if $\cos x = 0$, the ratio would be $-1$, not $3$): $$ \frac{\tan x + 1}{\tan x - 1} = 3 $$ Now, solve this algebraic equation for $\tan x$: $$ \tan x + 1 = 3(\tan x - 1) $$ $$ \tan x + 1 = 3\tan x - 3 $$ $$ 4 = 2\tan x \implies \tan x = 2 $$ To find $\sec^2 x$, we use the standard Pythagorean identity connecting tangent and secant: $$ \sec^2 x = \tan^2 x + 1 $$ Substituting $\tan x = 2$ yields: $$ \sec^2 x = (2)^2 + 1 = 5 $$

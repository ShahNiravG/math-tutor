1. A triangle has sides \(13,14,15\). Find the largest angle.

The largest angle is opposite the longest side, so it is the angle opposite side \(15\).

Let that angle be \(C\). By the Law of Cosines, \[ 15^2=13^2+14^2-2(13)(14)\cos C. \] Thus \[ 225=169+196-364\cos C, \] \[ 225=365-364\cos C, \] \[ 364\cos C=140, \] \[ \cos C=\frac{140}{364}=\frac{5}{13}. \] Hence \[ C=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{5}{13}\right). \]

So the largest angle is \[ \boxed{\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{5}{13}\right)} \approx \boxed{67.4^\circ}. \]

2. Two sides of a triangle are \(20\) and \(21\), and the included angle is \(60^\circ\). Find the third side, then the area.

Let the third side be \(c\). By the Law of Cosines, \[ c^2=20^2+21^2-2(20)(21)\cos 60^\circ. \] Since \(\cos 60^\circ=\tfrac12\), \[ c^2=400+441-840\cdot \frac12=841-420=421. \] So \[ \boxed{c=\sqrt{421}}. \]

For the area, use \[ [\triangle]=\frac12 ab\sin C. \] Thus \[ [\triangle]=\frac12(20)(21)\sin 60^\circ =210\cdot \frac{\sqrt3}{2} =105\sqrt3. \]

Therefore the answers are \[ \boxed{\sqrt{421}} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \boxed{105\sqrt3}. \]

3. A triangle has sides \(25,25,30\). Find its area without using a calculator.

This is an isosceles triangle with base \(30\). Drop an altitude from the apex to the base; it bisects the base into two segments of length \(15\).

Then the height \(h\) satisfies \[ h^2+15^2=25^2. \] So \[ h^2=625-225=400, \] \[ h=20. \]

Hence the area is \[ \frac12\cdot 30\cdot 20=300. \]

Therefore the area is \[ \boxed{300}. \]

4. A hiker walks \(12\) km due east, then \(5\sqrt2\) km on a bearing \(N45^\circ E\). How far is the hiker from the starting point?

Take east as the \(x\)-direction and north as the \(y\)-direction.

First displacement: \(12\) km due east gives \[ (12,0). \]

Second displacement: \(5\sqrt2\) km on a bearing \(N45^\circ E\) means \(45^\circ\) east of north, so its east and north components are equal: \[ 5\sqrt2\cdot \cos 45^\circ = 5, \qquad 5\sqrt2\cdot \sin 45^\circ = 5. \] Thus the second displacement is \[ (5,5). \]

Total displacement: \[ (12,0)+(5,5)=(17,5). \]

Distance from the start: \[ \sqrt{17^2+5^2}=\sqrt{289+25}=\sqrt{314}. \]

So the hiker is \[ \boxed{\sqrt{314}\text{ km}} \] from the starting point.

5. Point \(B\) is \(100\) miles from point \(A\) at bearing \(N60^\circ E\). A pilot mistakenly flies due east from \(A\) for \(50\) miles. How far is the pilot from \(B\)?

Place \(A\) at the origin, with east as \(x\)-axis and north as \(y\)-axis.

Bearing \(N60^\circ E\) means \(60^\circ\) east of north, equivalently \(30^\circ\) north of east.

So the coordinates of \(B\) are \[ (100\cos 30^\circ,\;100\sin 30^\circ) = \left(50\sqrt3,\;50\right). \]

The pilot flies due east \(50\) miles, so the pilot’s position is \[ P=(50,0). \]

Hence \[ PB^2=(50\sqrt3-50)^2+(50-0)^2. \] Factor out \(50^2\): \[ PB^2=50^2\left[(\sqrt3-1)^2+1\right]. \] Now \[ (\sqrt3-1)^2=3-2\sqrt3+1=4-2\sqrt3, \] so \[ PB^2=2500(5-2\sqrt3). \] Therefore \[ PB=50\sqrt{5-2\sqrt3}. \]

So the pilot is \[ \boxed{50\sqrt{5-2\sqrt3}\text{ miles}} \] from \(B\).

6. A triangle has sides \(29,29,42\). Find the exact value of \(\cos\theta\), where \(\theta\) is one of the base angles.

The triangle is isosceles with equal sides \(29,29\) and base \(42\). A base angle is the angle between sides \(29\) and \(42\), opposite the other side \(29\).

Apply the Law of Cosines: \[ 29^2=29^2+42^2-2(29)(42)\cos\theta. \] Cancel \(29^2\) from both sides: \[ 0=42^2-2(29)(42)\cos\theta. \] So \[ 2(29)(42)\cos\theta=42^2. \] Divide by \(42\): \[ 2(29)\cos\theta=42. \] Thus \[ \cos\theta=\frac{42}{58}=\frac{21}{29}. \]

Therefore \[ \boxed{\cos\theta=\frac{21}{29}}. \]

Solutions

1. Find the center and classify the conic: \[ 4x^2+9y^2-24x+54y+36=0. \]

Complete the squares: \[ 4x^2-24x+9y^2+54y+36=0 \] \[ 4(x^2-6x)+9(y^2+6y)+36=0 \] \[ 4\bigl((x-3)^2-9\bigr)+9\bigl((y+3)^2-9\bigr)+36=0. \] So \[ 4(x-3)^2-36+9(y+3)^2-81+36=0, \] hence \[ 4(x-3)^2+9(y+3)^2=81. \] Divide by \(81\): \[ \frac{(x-3)^2}{81/4}+\frac{(y+3)^2}{9}=1. \]

Thus the conic is an ellipse with center \[ \boxed{(3,-3)}. \]

2. A parabola has focus \((5,-1)\) and directrix \(x=-1\). Without writing the full equation first, find its vertex and the value of \(y\) when \(x=8\).

The directrix is vertical, so the axis is horizontal through the focus: \[ y=-1. \] The vertex is midway between the focus \((5,-1)\) and the directrix \(x=-1\). Since the distance from \(x=5\) to \(x=-1\) is \(6\), the midpoint in the horizontal direction is \[ x=\frac{5+(-1)}2=2. \] Hence the vertex is \[ \boxed{(2,-1)}. \]

The focal distance is \[ p=5-2=3. \] So the parabola has form \[ (y+1)^2=4p(x-2)=12(x-2). \] Now set \(x=8\): \[ (y+1)^2=12(6)=72, \] so \[ y+1=\pm 6\sqrt2, \] and therefore \[ \boxed{y=-1\pm 6\sqrt2}. \]

3. An ellipse has center \((2,1)\), passes through \((2,5)\), and has foci \((2,-2)\) and \((2,4)\). Find its equation.

The foci and center all have \(x=2\), so the major axis is vertical.

The center is \((2,1)\), and the foci are \((2,-2)\) and \((2,4)\). Thus \[ c=3. \]

Since the ellipse passes through \((2,5)\), and this point lies on the major axis, it must be a vertex. Its distance from the center is \[ a=|5-1|=4. \]

For an ellipse, \[ c^2=a^2-b^2. \] Thus \[ b^2=a^2-c^2=16-9=7. \]

With vertical major axis, the equation is \[ \frac{(x-2)^2}{b^2}+\frac{(y-1)^2}{a^2}=1. \] Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac{(x-2)^2}{7}+\frac{(y-1)^2}{16}=1}. \]

4. A hyperbola has asymptotes \[ y-3=\pm 2(x+1), \] and one vertex at \((1,3)\). Find its equation.

The asymptotes intersect at the center, so the center is \[ \boxed{(-1,3)}. \]

Since the asymptotes are \[ y-3=\pm 2(x+1), \] the hyperbola is centered at \((-1,3)\) and has either \[ \frac{(y-3)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(x+1)^2}{b^2}=1 \quad\text{with}\quad \frac ab=2, \] or \[ \frac{(x+1)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-3)^2}{b^2}=1 \quad\text{with}\quad \frac ba=2. \]

Now the given vertex is \((1,3)\), which lies horizontally \(2\) units from the center. So the transverse axis is horizontal, and \[ a=2. \] Hence the equation has form \[ \frac{(x+1)^2}{a^2}-\frac{(y-3)^2}{b^2}=1. \] Its asymptotes are \[ y-3=\pm \frac ba (x+1). \] Comparing with slope \(2\), we get \[ \frac ba=2. \] Since \(a=2\), it follows that \[ b=4. \]

Therefore \[ \boxed{\frac{(x+1)^2}{4}-\frac{(y-3)^2}{16}=1}. \]

5. Determine all real values of \(k\) for which \[ x^2-4x+y^2+2ky+4k-5=0 \] represents exactly one point.

Complete the squares: \[ x^2-4x=(x-2)^2-4, \] \[ y^2+2ky=(y+k)^2-k^2. \] So the equation becomes \[ (x-2)^2-4+(y+k)^2-k^2+4k-5=0, \] that is, \[ (x-2)^2+(y+k)^2=k^2-4k+9. \]

This is a circle centered at \((2,-k)\) with radius squared \[ r^2=k^2-4k+9=(k-2)^2+5. \] For the graph to represent exactly one point, we need \(r=0\), i.e. \(r^2=0\). But \[ (k-2)^2+5>0 \] for all real \(k\).

Hence there is no real \(k\) for which the equation represents exactly one point.

\[ \boxed{\text{No real value of }k} \]

6. A conic is given by \[ 9x^2-16y^2-54x-64y+19=0. \] Find its center, identify the conic, and determine its asymptotes mentally by transforming it to standard form.

Group and complete squares: \[ 9x^2-54x-16y^2-64y+19=0 \] \[ 9(x^2-6x)-16(y^2+4y)+19=0. \] Now \[ x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9,\qquad y^2+4y=(y+2)^2-4. \] So \[ 9\bigl((x-3)^2-9\bigr)-16\bigl((y+2)^2-4\bigr)+19=0. \] Thus \[ 9(x-3)^2-81-16(y+2)^2+64+19=0, \] hence \[ 9(x-3)^2-16(y+2)^2+2=0, \] or \[ 16(y+2)^2-9(x-3)^2=2. \] Divide by \(2\): \[ 8(y+2)^2-\frac92(x-3)^2=1. \] More usefully, \[ \frac{(y+2)^2}{1/8}-\frac{(x-3)^2}{2/9}=1. \]

So the center is \[ \boxed{(3,-2)}, \] and the conic is a hyperbola with vertical transverse axis.

For asymptotes, from \[ 16(y+2)^2-9(x-3)^2=2, \] we ignore the constant on the right and set \[ 16(y+2)^2-9(x-3)^2=0. \] Then \[ 4(y+2)=\pm 3(x-3), \] so the asymptotes are \[ \boxed{y+2=\pm \frac34(x-3)}. \]

So the final answers are:

  • Center: \(\boxed{(3,-2)}\)
  • Conic: \(\boxed{\text{hyperbola}}\)
  • Asymptotes: \(\boxed{y+2=\pm \frac34(x-3)}\).
